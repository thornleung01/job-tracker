Privacy

Job Tracker helps you follow your job applications by reading the job-related emails in your own mailbox. It runs entirely in your browser. There is no Job Tracker server.

This page covers the hosted edition at thornleung01.github.io/job-tracker. The local edition runs on your own PC, and its notes are in SETUP.md in the project.

What it reads

  • Gmail: read-only access (the gmail.readonly permission). The app can't send, delete or change emails.
  • Outlook: read-only access (the Mail.Read permission). The app can't send, delete or change emails.
  • Your email address and a stable account ID from Google or Microsoft (and your name, from Microsoft), so the app knows who is logged in and keeps each person's data apart. The name and address are shown on screen while you're logged in. The account ID is part of the name of your database in this browser.

The Google login asks only for your account ID and email address (the openid and email permissions), not your profile. The Microsoft login also asks for the profile permission, which is where your name comes from.

With Google, login and mail permission happen in one window, and you can untick the mail permission there. If you say no to it, or choose "Log in without connecting your mailbox", you still log in and can connect later in Settings. With Microsoft, one window logs you in and asks for the mail permission together. You choose whether to connect Gmail, Outlook, both or neither.

What it stores

  • Your applications, and the details the app picks out of job-related emails: company, role, stage, sender, subject, date, a link back to the message, and the start of the message text (up to 4,096 characters). This is saved in your browser's IndexedDB on this device, in a database that belongs to your account.
  • For email that isn't job-related, only its message ID and date, so it isn't read again. Its sender, subject and text aren't kept. If a rule you taught the app hides a message, that message is kept for about 30 days so you can bring it back.
  • What you add yourself: notes and steps on an application, an offer's salary and decision deadline, your resume and its versions, cover letters, the text of a job posting, which resume you sent with each application, contact edits, corrections you give about what is or isn't a job email, and the sender and domain rules the app learns from them. For each correction the app keeps the sender's address and domain and the email's subject, so it can keep learning from them.
  • If you choose a PDF, Word or text file for your resume, the app reads it on your device only. The file isn't uploaded, and only the text you check and save is kept.
  • When you dismiss an email in Needs review, say an application isn't one, or an application in Recently deleted is erased (with Delete now, Empty recently deleted, or after 30 days), the app erases that email's text, sender, subject, thread ID and link to the message. Only its message ID and date stay, the same as for any email that isn't about a job. The sender address and subject of a correction are kept as described above.
  • When you choose Delete application, nothing is erased yet. The application is marked as deleted (with the time you deleted it) and hidden, and it keeps its emails, notes, steps and contact edits for 30 days in Settings, Recently deleted. You can restore it from there. After 30 days, or when you choose Delete now or Empty recently deleted, it is erased as described above and its notes and steps go with it. The app erases the ones that have waited 30 days when it starts (a deletion time in the future, for example in an imported file, counts from that start instead). Mail that arrives for a deleted application, such as a reply in its thread or an update about the same company and role, is kept with it until you restore it or it is erased. The exception is a new email saying your application was received: that starts a new application, so applying again is never hidden. Mail the app isn't sure about goes to Needs review, as it does for any application. Two choices skip the 30 days and take effect at once: "Delete for good, keep emails in Needs review" removes the application and moves its emails to Needs review, and saying an application isn't one erases its emails as described above.
  • The address of each mailbox you connect, when it last synced, and your settings: appearance, table columns, how far back to read, your saved views (a name and the filters and sort it applies, up to 12) and your weekly goal, if you set one. All of it is saved in the same database.

The app also keeps a few small notes in your browser's own storage, apart from the database. None of them is a secret or holds an email, and a token is never among them.

  • Local storage, jobtracker:lastProvider: the provider you last logged in with (Google or Microsoft), so the login screen can remind you.
  • Local storage, jobtracker:carryover-done: a note that you've already brought earlier applications into an account.
  • Local storage, jobtracker:look: followed by the name of your database: your appearance choices (theme, density, corners and accent), so the page can paint in your look before the database opens. Because the name of your database contains your account ID, so does the name of this item.
  • Local storage, jobtracker:backup: followed by the name of your database: two times, when you last backed up (with Export data or Back up now) and when you last chose "Remind me in a week", so the backup reminder knows when to show. It is not part of the export. The name of this item contains your account ID too.
  • Session storage, jobtracker:local-banner-dismissed and jobtracker:carryover-dismissed: your Dismiss and Not now choices, until you close the tab.
  • Session storage, jobtracker:persist-asked: a note that the app has already asked your browser to keep its storage, so it asks once per tab session.
  • Session storage, jobtracker:offers-open: followed by the name of your database: whether the Compare offers card is open, until you close the tab.
  • Session storage, jobtracker:lastResume: followed by the name of your database: the id of the resume you last chose in "Resume sent", so the Add forms start with it, until you close the tab. The name of this item contains your account ID too.
  • A second IndexedDB database, jobtracker-backup-files: only if you choose a backup file (Chrome and Edge). It holds a reference to the file you picked (its name and a handle your browser gives the app), not your data. The file's contents go only to that file, and the app reads that file only to count the applications an earlier backup holds, on your device.

Access tokens are kept in memory only. The app never writes them to local storage, session storage, cookies or the address bar, and they are gone when you log out or close the tab. When the app asks Google for a fresh token without opening a window, it checks that the token is for the Gmail address you connected, and drops it if it isn't.

If this copy of Job Tracker was built without Google or Microsoft login set up, it opens without logging in and can't connect a mailbox. It keeps a local copy of your data in this browser only.

If you saved applications in this browser before you logged in, the app can offer to bring them into your account. The offer counts only the applications you haven't deleted, and anything in Recently deleted is copied along with them. It does this only when you choose to, never by itself. The earlier copy stays in this browser until you remove it with the button the app shows afterwards.

What leaves your device

Nothing is sent to any server run by the developer. Your browser talks only to Google and Microsoft, to log you in and to fetch your emails, and the emails are read and sorted on your device. With Google, your browser sends the access token to Google (oauth2.googleapis.com) to learn your account ID and email address. That check goes to Google, not to the developer. The login page loads Google's login script from accounts.google.com as soon as it opens, so Google can tell the page was opened. Microsoft's login window talks to login.microsoftonline.com when you log in. Google and Microsoft may set their own cookies on their own pages when you log in. No analytics, advertising or tracking is used, and your data isn't shared, sold or used to train any model.

When you paste a job link from Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby or SmartRecruiters, your browser asks that job board's public API for the job's title, company, location and description. The request contains only the board name and job number from the link, and it goes straight from your browser to the job board. The description is kept on the application as the job posting text, on your device only. The hosts are boards-api.greenhouse.io, api.lever.co and api.eu.lever.co, api.ashbyhq.com and jobs.ashbyhq.com, and api.smartrecruiters.com.

There is one case where a link from another site also causes a request. Some company career pages embed Greenhouse and add a gh_jid parameter (a Greenhouse job number) to their own web addresses. When a pasted link has one, the app guesses the Greenhouse board name from the page's own web address (for careers.example.com it tries example) and asks boards-api.greenhouse.io for that job, and for the board's company name if the job doesn't give one. The requests contain that guessed name and the job number, and nothing else from the link. A wrong guess just finds nothing. Every other link from another site is read on your device only, with no request.

The bookmarklet in Settings reads the job page you are viewing and opens this app with the details in the web address. It makes no network request of its own.

When you choose Add several links, the app reads up to 10 pasted links the same way as one: the requests are the ones described above for a single link (the same job boards, and the same guessed Greenhouse board for a gh_jid link), made up to 2 at a time, with each given up to 6 seconds. Nothing else is sent.

Draft follow-up opens a new message in Gmail or Outlook, in a mail account you have connected (never one you have disconnected). The recipient, subject and text (your company, the role, the day you applied and the recipient's first name in the greeting) are part of the link's web address, and for Gmail so is your own address, which tells Gmail which account to open. They go to Gmail (mail.google.com) or Outlook (outlook.office.com or outlook.live.com) only when you open the link, and only to the service that already has your mailbox. The app never sends the message and doesn't ask for permission to send: you read it, change it and send it yourself. Draft in Gmail and Draft in Outlook on a cover letter work the same way: the letter's text, cut to its first 3,000 characters, and the subject "Application for" the role are part of the link, and they go only to your own mail service when you open it. With no mail account connected, the buttons aren't shown.

Add to calendar and the calendar's download buttons save an .ics file on your device, made in your browser when you press the button. It holds the company, role and stage of an interview, the day (not a time) and a link back to the application in this app. The app doesn't send it anywhere. Importing the same day again updates the entry rather than adding a copy.

Export data and Export CSV in Settings each save a file on your device. The data export is a full copy you can import again, with your resumes, cover letters and job posting text. It includes applications, resumes and cover letters in Recently deleted (marked with when they were deleted) until they are erased. The CSV is a spreadsheet of your applications (company, role, stage, dates, posting link, offer details and the title of the resume sent) with no email text, no resume text and no job posting text, and it leaves out anything in Recently deleted. The app doesn't send either file anywhere.

In Chrome and Edge you can also choose a backup file in Settings, Your data. Back up now then writes the same full copy to that file, and only to that file, on your device. Before it replaces the file, the app reads it on your device to count the applications an earlier backup holds, and nothing from it is kept or sent. It never replaces a good backup with an empty one: it writes nothing when there is nothing worth saving (no applications, and no learned rules, contact edits, corrections, resumes or cover letters), and it asks you first when the file holds more applications than the new copy. The copy is written to a temporary file and only replaces your file once it is complete, so a failure leaves the earlier backup as it was. Once saved, a file is yours to handle, so keep it as private as you would any file with your job search in it.

When there is data in the app, it asks your browser to keep its storage rather than clear it when the device runs low on space (navigator.storage.persist()). That is a request to your own browser. Chrome and Edge decide without asking, Firefox may show you a prompt, and Safari may say no. Nothing is sent to anyone, and the app also asks the browser how much space it uses, to show you.

You can install the site as an app from your browser's menu. It then opens in its own window. It has no offline copy and still needs the network to read your mail, and installing it sends nothing extra.

Save as image, Copy image and Share on the Statistics page make a picture of the chart that shows totals only, with no company names or email addresses. Share hands the picture to your device's own share menu, and the app doesn't send it anywhere.

Use of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Deleting your data and removing access

  • To delete one application, choose Delete application. It stays in Settings, Recently deleted for 30 days, and you can restore it or choose Delete now to erase its emails, notes and steps for good. It is also erased by itself after 30 days.
  • To delete all your data, open Settings, go to Your data and choose Delete all my data. This empties your database in this browser, including anything in Recently deleted, and removes your backup reminder times and the reference to your backup file. You can also clear this site's data in your browser settings, which removes everything, including the small notes in local and session storage and the jobtracker-backup-files database.
  • Files you saved are yours to delete: exports, backup files and calendar files stay on your device until you remove them. They are not erased by Delete all my data. If you installed the site as an app, remove it from your browser or device as you would any app.
  • Log out clears tokens from memory. If you connected Gmail, it also revokes that Google access token. A Microsoft token is dropped from memory, but the permission stays until you remove it.
  • To remove Job Tracker's access to Google, open your Google account permissions and remove Job Tracker.
  • To remove its access to Microsoft, open your personal Microsoft account permissions, or My Apps for a work or school account, and remove Job Tracker.
